Publications

Journal Article

Soundtracks of Poverty and Development: Music, Emotions and Representations of the Global South

Journal of African Cultural Studies (2024)
Read the full article in The European Journal of Development Research

The article examines the soundtracks of fundraising videos produced by NGOs based in the UK, the USA and Canada to address questions about the ways that music structure and sound create emotional narratives about poverty and development. Analysis reveals how music is used to shape emotional responses to NGO appeals and reinforces persistent stereotypes about the global South as sad and frightening and the global North as the source of agency to solve problems of global poverty.

Pre-Publication Version

Soundtracks of poverty and development: Music, Emotions and the "Good life" in Lagos, Nigeria

Note: This is a pre-publication version. Content may differ from the final published article.

Download Pre-Publication PDF

This article explores the affective dimensions of urban poverty and development in Nigeria's commercial capital, Lagos. Drawing on long-term ethnographic fieldwork with students and teachers of a European classical music school situated in a low-income enclave of Lagos, it explores how young people navigate the city's precarious landscapes through music.

They use the sensorial affordances of classical music making and listening to create a "good life" that counters the stressful and chaotic soundscapes associated with the experience of poverty in the city.

Conference Presentation

Resonant Bodies, Intelligent Systems: A Critical Investigation of AI-Enhanced Somatic Pedagogy in Musical Improvisation & Pedagogy

Presented at the International Conference on Music Perception and Cognition, 2024

This research proposes a critical and hands-on investigation into the intersection of Artificial Intelligence (AI), somatic biofeedback, and musical improvisation.

Moving beyond the traditional pedagogical goal of technical "optimization," this project asks a more fundamental question: How does the introduction of an AI agent, capable of visualizing (using VR/AR technology) and responding (using bio-sensing technology) to the objective physiological state of a performer, alter the lived experience of improvisational agency? We propose a hybrid pedagogical model that centers the human sensory experience while leveraging machine learning for objective physiological monitoring.
